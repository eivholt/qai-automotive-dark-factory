# Model-gated process scene

`ADarkFactoryProcessScene` adds physical inspection parts only when the separate process controller initializes it. The original repeating 21-second welding preview and its legacy QA fixtures keep their imported geometry and timing. Process mode drives carriage position independently of robot pose, so a model gate can hold the body at the station without stopping cooling.

## Doors and source geometry

The locally purchased body is an unfinished shell. Its 29 connected body islands include two whole side frames (`SM_BodyPanel_0374` and `SM_BodyPanel_0383`), each approximately 65.69 x 585.54 x 201.19 cm, with two door apertures. They are not four detachable doors. The process helper preserves all 76 imported components and adds four authored door inserts, with lower sheet panels, open window frames, a character line and a handle. These are supplemental example geometry; they do not reconstruct production door thickness, hinges or crash structure.

The two door shapes are generated once in memory from simple prisms and bars. Each door shares one static mesh component and one thermal ID. They attach to the moving carriage through hidden bounding-box collision proxies. The front/rear names describe placement along the local body; the vehicle's final manufacturing configuration is not established.

Each new body has a seeded one-in-three anomaly probability, independent of model output, robot timing and image noise. An anomalous body chooses one of four doors and one of three states: visibly sagged/loose, falling, or missing. The loose state has an outward translation and skew. The fallen state detaches as the body reaches the station and uses a bounded 120 Hz swept-ballistic proxy, gravity, bounce and damping before resting on the floor. This is an illustrative rigid sheet drop, not flexible sheet-metal physics. The sweep excludes the moving carriage components while retaining the cell floor and platforms; valid contacts preserve the actual surface height. The render mesh and its broad collider share the same transform. Forward departure and return both end by removing loose pieces and resetting the next body's inserts.

Two fixed side cameras inspect the door apertures. Their initial poses are on opposite sides of the line and use the same 448 x 256 grayscale NIR contract. A view can be occluded or ambiguous; the controller must hold when the evidence is insufficient. A randomization label must never override the model's decision.

## Adhesive and weld cooling

Three narrow adhesive strips sit beside the three active welding sites. They are opaque GPU-rendered mesh surfaces, attached to the carriage and registered once in the imported thermal table. Each body receives independent initial temperatures of 76-108 C and exponential cooling time constants of 7-22 seconds:

`T(age) = 20 + (T_initial - 20) * exp(-age / tau)`

Adhesive emissivity is explicitly 1.0 in this illustrative contract, so surface and apparent temperature agree. Existing sheet-metal weld profiles retain emissivity 0.74. Offline metadata records both physical and apparent maximum weld temperatures; live gate decisions must use only sensor evidence. None of these temperatures or times establish an industrial adhesive cure schedule, weld qualification or safe production threshold.

The three existing weld patches and welding tools use the continuous process clock. A separate weld epoch is recorded when welding is authorized. The full per-robot 7.5-second heating interval and seeded delays remain; once the arms withdraw, the body can wait indefinitely while its weld fields and tools cool. New bodies reset only the body fields; tools retain residual warmth. Smoke aging also follows the process clock while a gate holds the robot pose; its bounded puffs expire after 3-4.4 seconds instead of remaining frozen during inspection. Physical sparks already advance from the unpaused simulation frame interval. Process motor heat integrates actual per-frame joint-angle travel, including the raised inspection arm, against the continuous clock. A rejected body does not inherit the old scheduled welding heat; temperatures persist across bodies and cool during holds. Explicit backward-clock QA resets the motor history. The old imported bulk heat-exchange guard remains enabled: the hollow car shell is not treated as a solid thermal box.

## Inspection arm and budgets

Robot R4 (index 3) is reserved for inspection. Its welding gun is hidden and excluded from emitters. The arm carries a physical camera housing and lens on its tool-roll joint. It moves from the authored withdrawn rest pose to the elevated inspection pose only during the adhesive and weld-cooling gates. A 0.8-second smooth transition follows the pausable simulation clock; during arrival, NIR door inspection, welding and either exit path it returns to rest. Thermal capture starts only after the arm reaches the established inspection endpoint, then retains the existing camera settle frames. It stays deployed while a thermal gate waits or reinspects. Pause freezes the current arm pose. A gimbal aims that same lens sequentially at each of the three weld sites. Its field of view fits the combined adhesive and weld bounds to the 448:256 image aspect ratio with a 25% margin; fitting only a fixed horizontal width can clip a vertically projected adhesive strip. The inspection camera pose is derived from the lens, with a site-sized field of view; it is separate from observer navigation.

Process geometry adds nine opaque thermal profiles: four doors, three adhesive strips and two camera components. With the existing imported scene, the expected total is 253 of the 255 available IDs. Door shapes are built once; the scene helper has no actor tick and allocates no render targets, image uploads or capture readbacks. The process controller owns capture scheduling. Existing source assets, runtime meshes and captures stay local and ignored.

## Reproducibility and inspection

`BeginBody` accepts explicit anomaly and door overrides for offline balanced evaluation. Explicit `SetCaptureVariation(seed)` calls add reproducible per-episode NIR camera offsets of up to 15 cm, target offsets of up to 4 cm, FOV changes of up to 2 degrees, and authored door color gains of 0.7-1.1. The ordinary interactive sensor poses/material remain unchanged unless this offline API is invoked. `ApplyFrame` receives controlled pose, simulation clock, carriage offset, body visibility, welding authorization and inspection-arm extension. Explicit offline thermal snapshots seek full extension before freezing; NIR snapshots use the resting arm. The calibrated thermal endpoint and camera fitting remain unchanged. Equal clocks preserve pause behavior. `BuildMetadata` is private ground truth for offline audits; it must never enter a model image, prompt or transport envelope.

The private source-aperture audit records input SHA-256 hashes and measured side-frame bounds. Native process QA should verify gate holds, all four door identities, loose-part cleanup on both exit paths, R4 emitter exclusion, continuous cooling, camera coverage, profile budget and unchanged legacy welding fixtures. Rendered inspection views are required to assess coverage; metadata and nonblank-image checks alone do not establish useful model evidence.

## Initial capture audit

The initial private 64-episode experiment contains 448 batches and 1,280 lossless images: 64 two-view door batches and 384 three-view thermal batches. All images are 448 x 256 and exact grayscale. Every thermal batch's visible maximum matches the apparent-temperature prediction within 0.537 of an 8-bit code, and the pixel-derived readiness label agrees with the recorded label. This establishes the illustrative rendering contract, not measured physical temperature or model accuracy. Door cases include 32 seated, 11 loose, 11 fallen and 10 missing bodies; native generated-mesh audits report no degenerate triangles or winding/normal mismatches. Whole episodes remain in one split.

The audit found a small physical placement defect in five negative-side fallen samples: the former 4 cm rest offset let a 4.1 cm handle penetrate the floor by 0.1 cm. Runtime contact clearance was increased to 4.35 cm. Those floor pieces lie outside the fixed NIR crops; the captured door-aperture evidence and readiness labels remain valid. Existing images and the original audit finding were retained rather than silently replaced. Private reports: `artifacts/process-dataset-physical-audit.json` and `artifacts/process-thermal-pixel-audit.json`.

The unchanged legacy welding regression also passes all nine independent suites (12,261 checks) against the corrected 1600 x 900 native capture in `artifacts/process-legacy-v2`. The first run used 1280 x 720 and was rejected by the existing image-contract checks; it was rerendered at the required resolution without relaxing any criteria.

The R4 parking update follows the frozen adapter evaluation on the original episode capture. Original validation/test numbers describe that retained dataset; the updated process is checked separately with live model inference. The change does not retrain against the reserved test episodes.

R4 parking validation passes the UE 5.8 build, 81 Python tests, 71 native policy checks (including 13 new arm/capture checks), the complete healthy/fault/healthy model-driven sequence, and the existing nine welding suites (12,261 checks).
